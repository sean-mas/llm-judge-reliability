   the interval of the difference rather than of two separate estimates.

   items judged by all three arms: 481

  pair                                    d kappa    95% CI of the difference     verdict
  tier1_paid - tier2_hosted_open           -0.034   [ -0.078, +0.007]      undecided
  tier1_paid - tier3_local                 +0.002   [ -0.044, +0.045]      undecided
  tier2_hosted_open - tier3_local          +0.036   [ -0.003, +0.074]      undecided

   'equivalent' means the whole interval lies inside +/-0.03 kappa points.

   one large enough to matter. That is a statement about power, not about
   the judges, and it is the honest reading of an overlapping interval.

========================================================================================
4. LEAVE-ONE-ANNOTATOR-OUT
========================================================================================

   The headline comparison is asymmetric as it stands: a judge is scored
   against a reference aggregated from several annotators, while the human
   figure is one annotator against one other annotator. Here each annotator
   is held out in turn and scored against a reference built from the
   remaining annotators on the same item, which is exactly the procedure
   applied to the judges. Both are then restricted to the same items.

   comparison set: 296 items carrying two or more annotators

  rater                           n      raw    kappa     TPR     TNR
  human, leave-one-out          713    0.823    0.314   0.877   0.474
  tier1_paid                    883    0.327    0.045   0.277   0.955
  tier2_hosted_open             850    0.421    0.030   0.396   0.734
  tier3_local                   859    0.342    0.012   0.309   0.750

   This subset excludes every item with no annotation at all, which under
   the reference rule is exactly the Consistent items. It is therefore
   harder than the full split for both raters, and the judge figures here
   are lower than the headline ones for that reason. The comparison
   between the two rows is the point, not their absolute level.

5. ROGAN-GLADEN TRANSFER, WITH UNCERTAINTY
========================================================================================

   true unfaithful rate on test: 68.6%
   2000 resamples, development resampled for TPR and TNR and test
   resampled for the flag rate, independently, in every draw.

  judge                 estimate                95% CI    true    error  unusable
  tier1_paid              69.3% [  43.1%, 100.0%]   68.6%    +0.6pp         0
  tier2_hosted_open       62.2% [  36.3%,  90.9%]   68.6%    -6.5pp         0
  tier3_local             53.8% [  32.5%,  79.9%]   68.6%   -14.8pp         0

   Which sample limits the precision? Holding one fixed and resampling
   the other separates the two contributions.

  judge                               both           test only            dev only
  tier1_paid                [43.1%,100.0%]      [53.4%, 84.4%]      [48.9%, 98.5%]
  tier2_hosted_open         [36.3%, 90.9%]      [48.3%, 77.7%]      [44.5%, 85.3%]
  tier3_local               [32.5%, 79.9%]      [40.3%, 67.3%]      [37.4%, 75.3%]

   The dev-only interval is nearly as wide as the full one, so the
   calibration set rather than the measured corpus is what limits precision.

   Estimates are clamped to [0, 1]; a prevalence cannot fall outside it.
   'unusable' counts resamples where Youden's J came out at or below zero,
   which makes the estimator undefined. A non-zero count is itself the
   finding: the correction sits close to the regime where it breaks.
